Purpose and scope

This runbook defines how to install, operate, configure, troubleshoot and maintain the Reuse Water System controller. It covers the controller firmware and the hardware connected directly to it. The washing machine, the plumbing design and the mains electrical installation are outside its scope.

Safety precautions

Isolation: switch off and lock out the valve and pump supply before working on any wiring. The Stop button is a software control and does not isolate power.

Electrical work: if the valves or pump run on mains voltage, the SSR load wiring must be installed by a qualified electrician to local regulations, with overcurrent protection and an RCD/GFCI.

Environment: mount the controller in an enclosure rated for damp locations, and keep low-voltage wiring separated from mains wiring.

Hygiene: grey water contains detergent residue and bacteria. Wear gloves when servicing the tank, sensors and valves. Stored water is not potable.

Supervision: do not leave the system unattended during commissioning or after a firmware change.

Quick start

For everyday use you only need the two buttons and the screen. After the controller is switched on, the screen shows a dog chasing a butterfly for about 4 seconds, then REUSE WATER / System ready, then READY.

  1. Check the screen shows READY and Press START.
  2. Load the washing machine and start its program as usual.
  3. Press START (the button labelled START). The screen changes to 1/4 WASH.
  4. Let it run. The screen shows the current step, 1/4 to 4/4. In the last step a countdown shows the time left.
  5. Done. The screen shows DONE and Cycle complete, then returns to READY.

To cancel at any time, press STOP (the button labelled STOP). The screen shows STOPPED for 4 seconds.

On a phone connected to the home Wi-Fi, open http://reusewater.local to see the same status, start or stop the cycle, and check how much water you have saved (section 11).

1. Overview

The controller runs a 4-step wash cycle (Fill, Drain, Rinse, Drain), reuses grey water from a storage tank for the first fill, and saves the rinse water back to the tank. It runs on an Arduino Nano ESP32, drives five solid-state relays (SSRs), reads two flow sensors and two tank level switches, and reports status on a 128x32 OLED.

Wash cycle summary

  1. Fill (1/4): the machine fills with tap water, or with tank water when the tank has water and the machine is demanding water. The tank-wash valve pulses every 10 s while tank water is used.
  2. Drain (2/4): soapy water is drained, not saved.
  3. Rinse (3/4): the machine fills with tap water.
  4. Drain (4/4): rinse water is saved to the tank until it is full. This step lasts 20 minutes, then the system returns to Idle.

Wiring requirements

SSR inputs: connect each output pin to the SSR's DC input (+) and GND to (−). The board must not drive solenoids or the motor directly.

Level switches: D8 and D9 use plain INPUT, so each switch needs an external pull-down (or pull-up, with the logic inverted in getWaterLevelTank()). A floating input gives random tank levels.

Buttons: wire between the pin and GND; no external resistor is needed.

Flow sensors: power them at the voltage the sensor and the board's inputs both accept. The Nano ESP32 inputs are 3.3 V only and not 5 V tolerant. If a sensor is powered from 5 V, use the diode and 10 kΩ pull-up shown in the wiring diagrams on D2 and D3. Do not use a resistor divider: with the sensor’s own pull-up it can leave the signal just above the pin’s switching level, so pulses drop out.

Pin D0 (tank-wash valve): D0 is also the board's RX0 serial pin. The sketch's Serial uses the USB port, so D0 is free, but do not use Serial0 in the same sketch. Fit a 10 kΩ resistor from D0 to GND so the SSR stays firmly off while the board starts. Always write D0 in the code, not a bare 0.

Pin D1 (buzzer, optional, from firmware 3.2): D1 is also the board's TX0 serial pin, so do not use Serial0. Use an active buzzer module (one that beeps when its input is HIGH): signal to D1, VCC to 3V3, GND to GND. The chip sends its own start-up messages on TX0 before the firmware runs, so the buzzer may chirp briefly at power-on or after a reset; this is normal. The first line of setup() drives D1 LOW. Always write D1 in the code, not a bare 1. Until 3.1 the buzzer was on D12.

Boot state: allSSROff() writes LOW to all five outputs in setup(), so every valve and the motor start off.

Pin numbers follow the Arduino labels printed on the board (Arduino IDE: Tools > Pin Numbering > By Arduino pin (default)). The GPIO column is for reference only. Source: Arduino Nano ESP32 full pinout.

Pump protection (from firmware 2.5): the pump runs only while water is actually flowing.

  • Fast cut-off: if no tap-sensor pulse arrives for 0.3 s, the pump stops and the tap valve opens at once. That happens when the machine closes its inlet, or when the pump delivers nothing (air lock, blocked intake, empty pipe). The pump gets 1 s after starting for water to flow.
  • No restart into a closed inlet: after a cut-off, the pump waits for the machine’s next draw.
  • Alert: after 3 starts with no water in one cycle, the controller sends a tank pump not pumping alert.
  • Hardware limit: firmware cannot remove the last 0.3 s. A pump with a built-in pressure switch, or a bypass back to the tank, protects it fully.

Interlocks (from firmware 3.0): applied every 20 ms in every state.

  • The fill valve (D5) and the tank-wash valve (D0) never open while the high float is wet. In step 4 the rinse water then goes to the drain, and saving stays off for the rest of that step.
  • The pump (D7) never runs while the low float is dry; the tap supplies the machine instead.
  • The pump cut-off above keeps working even if the main program is busy.

Tank level decoding

From firmware 3.0, the safety task reads both switches every 20 ms. Each switch reports its safe reading after 0.2 s (high float wet, low float dry) and the other reading only after 2 s, so a float bobbing at the mark does not switch valves on and off:

Tank-wash timing

While fillWashingMachineWithTankWater() runs, the wash valve opens immediately, stays open for TANK_WASH_OPEN_TIME (2 s), and reopens every TANK_WASH_PERIOD (10 s). The timer restarts on every state entry. Switching to tap water closes the valve; switching back to tank water in the same state continues the same 10 s cycle.

4. Buttons and operator controls

The system has two momentary buttons: Start begins a cycle from Idle, and Stop ends a cycle from any other state. Both are read every loop() pass by updateInputs() with a 20 ms debounce (DEBOUNCE_DELAY).

Operational notes

  • Start in any other state: ignored. A running cycle cannot be restarted without pressing Stop first.
  • Stop in Idle: ignored.
  • Stop paths: from Run and Fill 1/4 the state machine's own transition goes to Idle. From Drain 2/4, Rinse 3/4 and Drain 4/4, loop() also forces a reset with resetFSMToIdle(). Both end in the same Idle entry.
  • Buttons act while held: the debounce reports the button as pressed for as long as it is held, not once per press. Holding Start when a cycle ends starts the next cycle straight away.
  • Both buttons at once: do not press both buttons simultaneously. The resulting state depends on the order in which transitions are evaluated.
  • Stop is not an emergency stop. In Idle the tap and drain SSRs stay on (noSavingWater()). To make the installation safe, isolate the SSR and valve supply (see Safety precautions).

Standard operating procedure

  1. Check that the OLED shows READY / Press START.
  2. Load the washing machine and start its program.
  3. Press Start. The header changes to 1/4 WASH.
  4. No operator action is required while the cycle runs. Progress shows in the header (1/4 to 4/4); in step 4 a countdown shows the time left.
  5. When the OLED shows DONE / Cycle complete, the system is back in Idle.
  6. To abort, press Stop at any time.

Diagnostic procedure

  1. Set DEBUG_MODE to true in GreyWaterSystem.h and flash.
  2. Open the Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  3. Watch the state entries (Entering state: …), flow rates, tank level and SSR switching while you reproduce the fault.
  4. Set DEBUG_MODE back to false for normal use; the debug output slows the loop.

Developer note: DEBUG_PRINT(x) expands to an if without braces. Do not use it as the only statement before an else.

9. Commissioning and test checklist

Run these checks after installation, after any wiring change, and after flashing new firmware.

Before power-up

  • Every SSR input is wired to its pin from section 2, with a common GND
  • SSR load side and valve/motor supply are fused and switched off
  • Level switches have pull resistors; buttons go to GND
  • Flow sensor signal levels are safe for the board's inputs
  • If a buzzer is fitted: active buzzer module, signal on D1 (not D12)

Power-up and idle

  • OLED shows the Border Collie chasing the butterfly (about 4 s), then REUSE WATER / System ready for 2 s, then READY / Press START, with no "Cycle complete"
  • Pressing START during the animation ends it at once
  • Messages read clearly in the bold font at the normal viewing distance
  • All SSRs are off for the first moment, then tap and drain SSRs turn on in Idle
  • Tank icon follows the level switches when each float is lifted by hand (empty, half, full)
  • If a buzzer is fitted: at most a short chirp at power-on, then silent

Buttons

  • Start in Idle changes the header to STARTING, then 1/4 WASH
  • Stop during each step shows STOPPED / Cycle stopped for 4 s, then READY
  • Start during a running step has no effect

Full cycle

  • Fill 1/4 with the tank low uses tap water; the W icon stays outlined
  • Fill 1/4 with water in the tank switches to tank water once the machine draws water; the motor runs and W turns white for 2 s every 10 s
  • Drain 2/4 shows Not saving; the fill valve stays off
  • Rinse 3/4 uses tap water; the wash valve stays closed
  • Drain 4/4 saves water (fill valve on, drain off) and the countdown decreases each second
  • With the tank full, Drain 4/4 shows Tank full: drain
  • After 20 minutes the OLED shows DONE / Cycle complete, then READY; with the buzzer on, two short beeps

Faults

  • Disconnect the OLED and reset: the controller keeps running and the app's Service tab shows the screen as not working
  • Disconnect a flow sensor in Fill 1/4: the step waits, and Stop still returns to Idle

  1. Install the Arduino IDE (or arduino-cli) and the board package esp32 by Espressif Systems; select Arduino Nano ESP32.
  2. Install the libraries: Adafruit GFX, Adafruit SSD1306, AgileStateMachine and WiFiManager (from firmware 2.4 the FlowSensor library is no longer needed; the 3.2 screen fonts are part of the sketch).
  3. Keep the whole FSM_WashingMachineSystem folder together: the .ino and every .cpp and .h file in it. The folder name must stay FSM_WashingMachineSystem.
  4. Select the board and port, then Upload (over USB, or wirelessly, see section 11).
  5. Run the checks in section 9.

Software modification reference

  • Pins, timings and display settings: GreyWaterSystem.h (sections 2 and 7).
  • Relay combinations and interlocks: GreyWaterOutputs.h / .cpp (section 3).
  • OLED text: displaySetStatus() calls in each state's logic function, and the strings returned by checkAndFillWashingMachine() and checkAndFillWaterTank(). Every screen text is a TRS("English", "Español") pair; run tools/check_screen_text.py after changing one.
  • Screen layout, fonts and start-up animation: GreyWaterDisplay.cpp, FontHelvBold12.h, FontHelvBold14.h, BootAnimation.h.
  • States and transitions: setupStateMachine() in the .ino (section 5).

11. Wi-Fi features (firmware 2.0)

Firmware 2.0 adds a phone dashboard, alerts, water statistics and wireless updates; the wash cycle runs exactly the same when Wi-Fi is missing. From firmware 3.2 the app is called Reuse Water System (Spanish: Sistema de Reúso de Agua).

First-time Wi-Fi setup

  1. On first boot, after the start-up animation, the screen shows ReuseWater-Setup and the setup key, for example Key gw-a1b2c3 (in Spanish, only the key). The key is unique to each board (from firmware 3.0) and is also printed on the USB serial monitor.
  2. On a phone, join the Wi-Fi network ReuseWater-Setup with that key. Only if the screen is missing or broken is the key the old shared password greywater.
  3. A setup page opens (otherwise browse to 192.168.4.1). Choose the home network and enter its password.
  4. The screen shows the controller's IP address for 6 seconds.
  5. Open http://reusewater.local (or that IP address) and sign in as admin, password greywater.
  6. In Settings, set new passwords and an alert topic (see Phone alerts).

The setup page closes after 3 minutes and the system then runs offline. To open it again, for example after changing the router, hold STOP for 5 seconds while the screen shows READY.

From firmware 3.0 the setup page only sets the Wi-Fi network and shows information: it cannot install firmware or erase the controller, because it has no password of its own.

Name change (3.2): until firmware 3.1 the app was at greywater.local, the setup network was GreyWater-Setup and the update port was greywater. After the 3.2 update they are reusewater.local, ReuseWater-Setup and reusewater. The IP address, passwords, settings and water history do not change; update any bookmark or home-screen shortcut.

  • Offline: the controller cannot be reached.

Keeping the water history

The Water use history survives firmware updates. From firmware 2.8 it is protected in three places:

WhereWhat it does
The controllerStores the history twice (main record and backup copy), saves it again before every wireless update, and never overwrites a record it cannot read
Every phone that opens the appKeeps its own copy, updated automatically. If the controller ever comes back without history, the Water use tab shows the phone’s copy and a Restore to controller button
A history fileDownload history file on the Water use tab. Restore from file brings it back, even onto a replaced or fully erased controller

If the Water use tab is empty after an update:

  1. Open the app on a phone that was used before the update. Use the same address as usual: reusewater.local and the IP address keep separate copies (before 3.2 the address was greywater.local).
  2. On the Water use tab, press Restore to controller. If no phone has a copy, use Restore from file with a downloaded history file.
  3. Check the Service tab: Water history should read Restored from the app.

Avoid erasing the controller’s memory: a normal upload keeps the history. A full flash erase removes it, together with the settings and the Wi-Fi network. That covers Burn Bootloader, recovery mode, uploading with another board or partition scheme selected, and Erase All Flash. Download a history file before any such work.

Manual tank wash

Use it to rinse the storage tank between washes. The wash valve opens and closes by itself as soon as the high-level switch reports that the tank is full.

  1. Check that the dashboard shows Ready and the tank is not full.
  2. In the Storage tank panel, press Wash tank. The screen on the controller shows TANK WASH and the time left before the safety limit.
  3. Wait. When the high-level switch reports water, the valve closes and the dashboard shows Tank full: wash valve closed.
  4. To end it early, press Stop washing on the dashboard or STOP on the controller. Starting a cycle also ends it.

Wireless firmware updates

  1. Stop any running cycle.
  2. In the Arduino IDE, choose Tools > Port and the network port named reusewater (a board still running 3.1 or earlier shows greywater).
  3. Upload and enter the update password.
  4. The controller switches every valve and the pump off, shows UPDATING, then restarts with the start-up animation.

Security

  • Change both default passwords on first use.
  • Keep the dashboard on the home network; do not forward router ports to it.
